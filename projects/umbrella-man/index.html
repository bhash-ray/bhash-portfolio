Concept

The Umbrella Man is a narrative adventure game concept about grief, isolation and the difficulty of reconnecting with the world after loss. It was developed as a stop-motion trailer that introduces the world, its central character and the visual language of the proposed game, using handcrafted characters, physical sets and miniature environments.

Core Idea

“A man without a voice in a world full of mouths.”

The Design Challenge

“How do you make grief visible without relying on dialogue?”

The Design Approach

The protagonist has no mouth. Everyone around him has an exaggerated, oversized one. Grief often comes with a flood of advice, consolation and opinions, sometimes from people who do not understand what the grieving person is going through. The contrast between his silence and their noise carries the emotional conflict through image rather than speech.

His umbrella is more than an everyday object. It is his shield, his escape and the one thing he carries through a world he no longer knows how to engage with.

Silence→Noise→Shelter

Narrative Structure

A man withdraws from the world after a personal loss. As he moves through different environments, the umbrella shields him from people, helps him evade obstacles, and lifts him across spaces he cannot cross on foot, including rivers. The journey leads him to a place where he can spend time with his wife. In the proposed full game, he would eventually find his voice and learn to let go of the umbrella.

Inspirations

Research & Inspiration

Stop-motion references

Coraline informed the atmosphere, proportions and lighting. Tim Burton’s films, especially The Nightmare Before Christmas, shaped the exaggerated silhouettes and darkly whimsical mood. A felt-based Thumbelina found on YouTube pushed experiments in how soft materials can stand in for water.

Learning by watching

There was no formal research study. The team watched short films and stop-motion works to understand lighting, camera angles, framing and composition, then tested materials by hand.

Story

A man withdraws from the world after a loss. People with oversized mouths offer opinions and consolation that never reach him. His umbrella shields him, carries him through the air and across rivers, and the journey ends where he can spend time with his wife.

Art Direction

Stylised characters, miniature environments and handcrafted materials make a surreal, storybook-like world. The physical imperfections and tactile quality of handmade animation were kept on purpose, rather than chasing a polished digital finish. Each set was built with care for scale, material, texture, lighting and composition.
